Convert Case and Text Mechanic alternatives

If you've landed here you're probably using one of the long-standing online text-tool sites and wondering whether something else is better. This is an honest comparison, including the part where one of them beats Textbench outright.

Short version: Textbench runs every tool in your browser with no rate limit and no paid tier. Convert Case has a considerably bigger catalogue. Text Mechanic caps free use per hour.

Text Mechanic: the hourly cap

Text Mechanic offers roughly 30+ tools and needs no signup, but free use is metered — hit it enough in a session and you get "Limit of 4 free tasks per hour exceeded", with Text Mechanic Pro sold as the way to remove it (checked July 2026).

That limit also tells you something about the architecture: work you can rate-limit per hour is work happening on a server, which means your pasted text is being sent to one. For a case conversion that's unremarkable; for an API response with a token in it, or an internal document, it's worth knowing.

Textbench has no per-hour cap because there's nothing to cap — the work happens on your machine, so there's no server cost to ration and no queue to stand in.

Convert Case: genuinely bigger

Convert Case is free, needs no signup, and covers roughly 150+ tools across case conversion, code and data formatting, image conversion, Unicode font styles and random generators. That is more than Textbench's 63, and if you want image format conversion or a specific generator that isn't here, it may simply have it and Textbench won't. Both sites carry ads.

Where Textbench differs is what happens to your text. Convert Case doesn't state whether it processes client- or server-side; Textbench does everything locally and you can verify it — open the Network tab while you use a tool and nothing carrying your text goes out, and the tools keep working with your connection off.

What Textbench does differently

  • Everything client-side. Paste an API response, a config file, or an internal document without handing it to a third party. The one exception is documented on its own page: the QR generator calls an external image service, and says so.
  • No caps, no Pro tier. No per-hour limit, no monthly counter, no feature held back for a paid plan.
  • Works offline. It's installable and the tools keep running with no connection.
  • A guide on every tool page covering the parts that actually bite — why uppercasing German ß isn't reversible, why MD5 isn't for passwords, why Base64 isn't encryption.
  • Fast to get around. Cmd/Ctrl+K opens a search over all 63 tools from any page.

Which to use

  • Sensitive text — anything with credentials, customer data or unreleased work in it: use a client-side tool. That's Textbench's whole design.
  • A tool Textbench doesn't have — image conversion and some niche generators aren't here. Convert Case's catalogue is larger; use it.
  • Bulk or repetitive work — an hourly cap gets in the way fast, so a local tool suits better.
  • You just need one quick conversion — honestly, any of the three will do it. Start here if you'd rather it not leave your browser.

Textbench is not affiliated with Convert Case or Text Mechanic. Their features, limits and pricing were checked in July 2026 and may have changed since — the fastest way to be sure is to look at their sites.
